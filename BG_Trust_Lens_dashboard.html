Trust & Readability Audit

How does Baillie Gifford's website language compare with its rivals on the signals that build a young investor's trust, and is it genuine or just positive? Pick a group and run.

Public web pages only · no personal data

Each provider is scored across four page types (by role, not page title). Enter one or more URLs per cell. Tick N/A only when a provider genuinely has no such page; N/A pages are removed from the coverage check so they do not penalise the score.

How the scores work
  • Trust dimensions (0–100 each). The engine operationalises the three antecedents of trustworthiness from Mayer, Davis & Schoorman (1995): Ability (competence signals), Benevolence (customer-care signals) and Integrity (honesty and disclosure signals). Weighted cues are counted per sentence with a 3-token negation window, normalised per 1,000 words, then squashed to 0–100:
    dim = 100 · (1 − e^(−0.18 · rate))
  • Trust Index. The mean of the three dimensions, minus half the hype penalty (trust-breaker cues, capped at 40):
    TSI = mean(A, B, I) − 0.5 · min(penalty, 40)
  • Credibility controls. Every website is written to sound positive, so these sit alongside the Trust Index and keep it honest: Candour = risk and downside admitted per 1,000 words; Substantiation = share of claims carrying evidence (a number, £, %, FCA/FSCS); Spin = puffery ÷ (puffery + candour).
  • Readability — a separate module, never mixed into the Trust Index. Flesch Reading Ease and Flesch–Kincaid grade, plus finance jargon and novice-blocking hard-jargon per 1,000 words.
  • The receipts. Open "words behind the score" on any card to see the exact cues that fired. Every number traces back to visible language.
Public web copy only. Trust dimensions follow Mayer, Davis & Schoorman (1995); readability is reported as a separate module. Scores normalised 0–100 per 1,000 words. Readability uses a standard syllable estimator, so values can differ slightly from a full statistical package. A single short page can understate a provider. Not investment advice.

Methodology note

The Trust Lens dictionary operationalises the trust model of Mayer, Davis & Schoorman (1995). Every term is transparent and editable in open CSV files, weighted by importance, with negation handled and cross-checked against the Loughran–McDonald financial dictionary. Public web copy only.
